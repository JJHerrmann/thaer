The newest of the five real-world-grounded blocs on the Thaer map, surfaced while extending the Dreikronerreich Kingdom Map east past Europe. Where the Sunni Caliphate federates a real patchwork of separate Sunni states under a shared legal/theological tradition, the Shia Bloc is currently just one real polity — Qajar Persia — because that's genuinely how the religious map looked in 1815: Twelver Shia Islam was, and still is, a minority tradition within the wider Muslim world, concentrated almost entirely in Persia itself after the Safavids made it the state religion by force in the 16th century.

That singularity is worth taking seriously rather than papering over. Persia isn't a smaller version of the Sunni Caliphate's structure — it's a genuinely different kind of bloc: one state carrying an entire distinct sectarian identity on its own, wedged geographically between the Ottoman-led Sunni world to its west, Sunni Afghanistan to its east, and Orthodox Russia to its north. Real history gave this exact spot a lot of friction for exactly that reason.

Open

  • No capital, named shah/ruler, or internal court structure yet — the real Qajar dynasty is the obvious placeholder if this gets developed further.
  • Whether the bloc stays a single country or grows: real history doesn't hand this setting an easy second Shia-majority state the way Sunni Islam's spread gives the Caliphate its federated cast — any expansion here would need real justification, not just added for symmetry with the other four.
  • Relationship to the Sunni Caliphate specifically — real Ottoman-Persian rivalry ran for centuries and would be a natural throughline if this bloc gets a fuller writeup.
